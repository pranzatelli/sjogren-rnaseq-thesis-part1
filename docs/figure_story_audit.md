## Interpretation of the New Comparisons

The expression-by-genotype figure answers the old res9 question more directly but does not establish an eQTL: it uses RNA genotype calls and expression from the same samples, and cohort-stratified distributions remain observational. The evidence matrix now prints every pooled q across the five phenotypes for each q<0.25 variant/gene row; its DE panel shows matching-contrast fold changes when padj<0.05. The GO dot plot compares DE and MORFR terms, while explicitly noting that variant-overlap gene sets had no term at within-set q<0.05. This is not evidence that no variant is functional.

The Kendall/MORFR concordance result supports the original motivation: top-25 overlap ranged from 37.6x to 354x the random expectation, and top-1,000 overlap remained 1.44x to 4.50x expected. Within the tree top-1,000, tie-aware Kendall tau-b was 0.158 to 0.282. This is partly expected by design because MORFR feature sampling is weighted by training-only Kendall scores; `var1` uses all samples, so the comparison is informative about internal agreement but is not independent validation. Neither Kendall rho-squared nor the depth-weighted split score should be described as classical variance explained.

The current gene-count matrix was already adjusted with ComBat-seq using cohort as batch and SjD diagnosis as the protected group. A multivariate linear projection on adjusted log2(CPM+1) values gave partial R²=0.0067 for cohort and 0.0157 for diagnosis after controlling for the other factor. The visible cohort grouping is therefore not evidence that unadjusted cohort variance still dominates these PCA dimensions; further pyCombat was not applied.

## Biological Assessment

The evidence supports a real phenotype-associated expression/prediction signal, but it does not yet establish a novel variant-driven mechanism. DE and fold-safe MORFR share more genes than a random-set baseline, and their enriched GO terms overlap heavily; however, both analyses reuse these subjects and phenotypes, MORFR uses Kendall-weighted feature sampling, and the common GO categories are mostly broad immune or cellular terms. No variant-overlap gene set had a GO term at within-set q<0.05.

Among five q<0.05 variant-phenotype results, HLA-DQA1 has the clearest descriptive expression consistency: ALT carriers have higher mean expression in both cohorts and both SSA status strata. HLA-DQA1 is also an established immune-locus candidate, so this is not by itself novel biology. GSR and SLC17A5 carrier-expression differences reverse across cohort/status strata; METTL7A differences are small; IGHG3 lacks BRA reference carriers and has few MSG references. RNA-derived genotypes and same-sample expression limit all these comparisons.

The RF MORFR gene set has an RF-specific RISC/miRNA enrichment. MIR376C and MIR494 occur among the term's expressed-background genes and rank 23 and 21 in the RF tree ranking; both also have RF DE support. This is the clearest novel hypothesis surfaced by the replacement figures, but it is precisely the small-RNA story that needs orthogonal replication rather than a causal claim. A matched-DNA/independent-cohort variant validation and independent small-RNA measurement would materially change confidence.

## Reading the MORFR Pair

`morfr_variant_overlap.pdf` has four plotted gene-target rows but only three unique genes: HLA-DQA1/SSA (rank 744), IGHG3/RF (rank 795), and CD74/RF (rank 181) plus CD74/Focus Score (rank 620, cross-target). Marker color is the gene's best pooled variant-q tier: red q<0.05, orange 0.05<=q<0.10, blue 0.10<=q<0.25. Filled circles are ranks for a phenotype associated with that gene's variant; open squares are ranks for a different phenotype. Thus the blue CD74/Focus Score square is cross-target, while its blue RF circle is target-matched.

In `kendall_morfr_rank_concordance.pdf`, the top-k hypergeometric tail test asks whether the intersection exceeds random selection from the shared feature universe. Its q-value is not predictive fit. Kendall tau-b is the rank association between the full-data Kendall score and saved MORFR order among MORFR's top 1,000 genes; positive tau means a higher score tends to pair with a smaller (better) rank. Overall target corr² in the companion MORFR panel is the held-out prediction metric. Kendall and MORFR agreement is partly expected because training-fold MORFR feature sampling itself uses Kendall weights, so this is internal correspondence, not independent validation.
